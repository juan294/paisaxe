## Post Content

### Subtitle/Kicker
*How 2 weeks of nights and weekends became a different way to discover a corner of Spain most people have never heard of*

---

There's a region on Spain's northern coast that most travellers skip entirely.

Asturias. Mountains that rival the Alps. A coastline where cliffs drop straight into the Atlantic. Cider poured from above your head. Churches older than most countries. The original Camino de Santiago. And almost no one there.

It already has beautiful websites. Stunning photos. Carefully written guides. There's no shortage of information about Asturias.

So when I sat down to build something for it, I didn't want to add more information. I wanted to change how you discover what's already there.

### The Idea

What if instead of reading about a place, you could talk to it?

Not read a paragraph about a cathedral—ask the cathedral a question. Not scroll through a list of beaches—have a conversation with one. Not plan your next stop from a map—let the place you're standing in guide you to what comes next.

That's [Paisaxe](https://paisaxe.es)—"landscape" in the local Asturian language.

### How It Works

Pick a place. A monument. A mountain trail. A restaurant. A cable car. A hidden village. If it's part of the story of Asturias, you can talk to it.

By chat. Or by voice.

And instead of the questions you'd type into a search engine, you ask the questions you'd ask a local:

"Is this worth seeing on a rainy day?"
"What should I notice when I'm here?"
"Where should I go next if I have 2 hours?"
"What's the story behind this place?"

The conversation follows your curiosity. Not a predetermined route. Not a ranked list. Just discovery at your own pace.

### Why Conversation Changes Everything

When you read a travel guide, you get information. Useful, but flat. You scan, bookmark, move on.

When you talk to a place, something different happens. You follow threads. You ask "tell me more." You go deeper on what catches your attention and skip what doesn't.

It changes the rhythm from planning to discovering.

Less "what are the top 10 things to do" and more "what should I pay attention to right now."

Less checklist. More connection.

### Why Asturias

I could have tried to build this for anywhere. But the best experiences come from depth, not breadth.

Asturias is perfect for this because:

**It rewards curiosity.** The best moments in Asturias aren't the famous landmarks—they're the things you stumble into. A Romanesque church hidden behind a farm. A viewpoint you almost drove past. A sidrería where the owner tells you stories while pouring cider from shoulder height.

**It's under-explored.** While millions crowd into Barcelona and Madrid, Asturias stays quiet. The people who go there tend to be the ones who slow down and pay attention.

**It has layers.** Pre-Roman history, medieval pilgrimage routes, industrial heritage, fishing villages, alpine landscapes—all compressed into a small region. Conversation is the right way to peel back those layers.

### What I Learned Building It

I spent 2 weeks on this—nights and weekends. A passion project, not a business plan.

The biggest lesson wasn't technical. It was about what happens when you change the format.

When people read a guide, they optimize. What's the best? What's the closest? What has the most stars?

When people talk to a place, they wander. They follow their mood. They ask the question they actually have, not the question a search engine expects.

"I'm tired and it's raining—what's something calm I can do nearby?"

No travel guide has a section for that. But a conversation handles it perfectly.

### For Anyone Planning a Trip to Asturias

Or for anyone who's curious about a place they've never heard of.

Paisaxe is free. No login, no ads. Just pick a place and start talking.

Chat if you're planning from home. Voice if you're there and want your hands free.

And if you try it—tell me what you spoke to first. A beach? A peak? A hidden village?
